𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗔𝗖𝗜𝗗 𝗧𝗿𝗮𝗻𝘀𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀 𝗶𝗻 𝗗𝗮𝘁𝗮𝗯𝗮𝘀𝗲𝘀? Ever wondered how your bank transfers or online orders never go half-done even if something crashes in the middle? Hi everyone, I’m Ashmita 👋 Let’s talk about the real hero working silently in the background: ACID transactions in databases. Imagine you’re building an e-commerce app. A customer places an order. Your app needs to deduct stock, charge the card, and update accounts, all in one go. But what if the payment fails after the stock is deducted? Or the app crashes halfway? This is where ACID properties step in and ensure everything runs smoothly:  • 𝗔𝘁𝗼𝗺𝗶𝗰𝗶𝘁𝘆: All steps succeed together or the system cancels everything.  • 𝗖𝗼𝗻𝘀𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝘆: Data remains valid before and after the transaction.  • 𝗜𝘀𝗼𝗹𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻: Transactions do not interfere with each other.  • 𝗗𝘂𝗿𝗮𝗯𝗶𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆: Once a transaction is complete, it is safely saved even if the system fails. From transaction logs to rollback mechanisms and isolation levels like Serializable, databases are built to handle complex processes without losing data or leaving it incomplete. These principles power everything from banking systems to e-commerce platforms. If you are exploring backend development or diving into system design, ACID is a core concept you must understand.
