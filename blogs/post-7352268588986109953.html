Ashmita Pandey · Jul 19, 2025 · Backend Engineering · Reliability

𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝗘𝗿𝗿𝗼𝗿 𝗛𝗮𝗻𝗱𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗠𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿𝘀 ? Error handling is not optional. It can never be ignored because unexpected situations always occur in real-world applications, and no one wants their app to break when something goes wrong. If you’ve used apps like Swiggy, Zomato, or Amazon, you’ve probably noticed how smooth and reliable they feel. That’s not because everything always works perfectly. It’s because these apps are designed to handle failures properly. In the screenshot below, I searched for a completely random and meaningless input on Swiggy. Instead of crashing or showing a confusing error, Swiggy handled it gracefully with a clear message: “𝗪𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝘂𝗹𝗱𝗻’𝘁 𝗳𝗶𝗻𝗱 𝗮𝗻𝘆 𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘂𝗹𝘁𝘀 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝘀𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗰𝗵.” This is what proper error handling looks like. The app was ready to deal with the unexpected without failing. 𝗥𝗲𝗮𝗹-𝗪𝗼𝗿𝗹𝗱 𝗘𝘅𝗮𝗺𝗽𝗹𝗲𝘀 𝗼𝗳 𝗚𝗼𝗼𝗱 𝗘𝗿𝗿𝗼𝗿 𝗛𝗮𝗻𝗱𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗴:  𝟭. 𝗢𝘂𝘁-𝗼𝗳-𝗦𝘁𝗼𝗰𝗸 𝗜𝘁𝗲𝗺 𝗼𝗻 𝗔𝗺𝗮𝘇𝗼𝗻 When a product is marked as "Currently unavailable," Amazon does not let you add it to your cart. The app does not crash. It simply displays the message: "This item is currently unavailable."  𝟮. 𝗠𝗶𝘀𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗥𝗲𝗾𝘂𝗶𝗿𝗲𝗱 𝗙𝗶𝗲𝗹𝗱𝘀 𝗼𝗻 𝗦𝘄𝗶𝗴𝗴𝘆 Try placing an order without entering your delivery address. Swiggy does not fail. It prompts you to complete the missing details before proceeding. 𝟯. 𝗙𝗮𝗶𝗹𝗲𝗱 𝗣𝗮𝘆𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 𝗣𝗿𝗼𝗰𝗲𝘀𝘀 Cancel a payment midway on Swiggy or Amazon. Instead of freezing or failing, the app shows: "Payment failed. Please try again." Even critical failures are handled smoothly. 𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝗥𝗼𝗯𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝗘𝗿𝗿𝗼𝗿 𝗛𝗮𝗻𝗱𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗜𝘀 𝗘𝘀𝘀𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗶𝗮𝗹:  • Prevents sudden crashes and keeps your app stable  • Improves user experience with clear, helpful message  • Simplifies debugging with meaningful error logs  • Protects data integrity in critical systems  • Prevents security risks by not exposing sensitive system details  • Reflects engineering maturity by preparing for real-world issues Always ask these questions while building any feature  • What could go wrong?  • Am I handling that situation properly?
