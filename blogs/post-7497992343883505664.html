The Genius Behind Instagram’s “Infinite Scroll” 👀

Ashmita Pandey · Aug 25, 2026 · System Design · Video · CDNs

The Genius Behind Instagram’s “Infinite Scroll” 👀 You keep scrolling. And scrolling. And somehow, there’s always another Reel ready for you. That’s what makes infinite scroll feel so effortless. You don’t have to wait for the next piece of content. You just keep going. Read till the end for one interesting fact 👀 So let’s break down how this works. First, the recommendation algorithm tries to predict what you’re likely to watch next. It can use signals like: → What you’ve watched before → What you’ve liked, shared, or saved → How long you watched a Reel → What you tend to skip → What similar users are engaging with Based on these signals, it can rank a bunch of Reels and decide which ones are most relevant to you. And then comes the interesting part. The app can prefetch parts of the likely next Reel while you’re still watching the current one. A simplified flow looks something like: Recommendation → Prefetch → CDN → Local buffer → Play Instead of waiting for you to swipe and then downloading the video, the app can start fetching it in advance from a nearby CDN. The video is delivered in smaller chunks, so while you’re watching the current Reel, the next one can already be getting downloaded in the background. So when you swipe: → The video is already partially downloaded → The player already has data in its buffer → Playback can start almost immediately But there’s another interesting problem. What if Instagram preloads a Reel you never watch? Doing this for every possible Reel would waste a huge amount of bandwidth. So the system has to balance: Relevance vs. playback speed vs. bandwidth vs. battery And then there’s adaptive bitrate streaming, which can adjust the video quality based on your network conditions. So that tiny swipe that feels instantaneous? Behind it, you have: • Recommendation algorithms • Prefetching • CDNs • Caching • Video chunking • Adaptive bitrate streaming • Bandwidth optimization Simple to use. Not so simple to build. And if you really read till the end, here’s the interesting fact 👀 Instagram doesn’t need to download the entire Reel before you can start watching it. It can load enough of the video into your buffer to start playback, while the rest continues downloading in the background. So by the time you swipe, the video feels like it was already waiting for you. And that’s how the infinite scroll keeps rolling. What other everyday feature should we break down next?
