One grey tick. Two grey ticks. Two blue ticks.

Ashmita Pandey · Oct 30, 2026 · System Design · Distributed Systems · Messaging

One grey tick. Two grey ticks. Two blue ticks. Three tiny icons on a chat app, and each one is a separate message travelling back to your phone. Stripped down, the flow looks like this. You hit send. Your phone pushes the message with an id it generated itself: {"type":"msg","id":"3EB0A1","to":"user_42","body":"on my way"} The server stores it and answers: {"type":"ack","id":"3EB0A1","status":"sent"} That's tick one. It only means the server has it. Your friend's phone could be switched off in a drawer. When their phone comes online and pulls the message, it replies: {"type":"receipt","id":"3EB0A1","status":"delivered"} The server relays it to you. Tick two. They open the chat, and one more goes out: {"type":"receipt","id":"3EB0A1","status":"read"} Blue. The quiet hero here is that client-generated id. Mobile networks drop packets all day, so phones retry. The server dedupes on the id, which means a retry never becomes a duplicate message, and every receipt knows exactly which bubble to update. It's the same idea I used to stop a pipeline from paying for the same LLM call twice: let the producer name the thing, and make every step idempotent on that name. So when someone sits on two grey ticks for an hour, the server did its job. They just haven't opened it 😄 #SystemDesign #DistributedSystems #BackendEngineering
