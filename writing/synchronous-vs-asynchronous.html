Synchronous vs Asynchronous Communication

Originally published Jul 18, 2025

  • Distributed Systems
  • Messaging
An on-site adaptation of Ashmita’s earlier article, not a verbatim republication.

An image upload has two separate outcomes: accepting the file and finishing the resize. Treating them as one operation makes the user wait for both. Separating them gives the application a way to report progress while a worker performs the expensive part.

Wait when the result drives the next decision

In a synchronous exchange, the caller needs the response before it can continue that operation. A login screen, for example, needs to know whether the credentials were accepted. This interaction is often easier to follow in a trace, but the caller now depends on the service replying within a useful deadline. A synchronous API does not necessarily mean a server thread sits idle waiting.

Queue work whose result can come later

An asynchronous design can acknowledge the upload, queue a resize job, and let the worker finish independently. The harder questions are how to show job status, how to retry without duplicating side effects, and what to do with jobs that keep failing. Choose the boundary based on what the caller must know now and what it can learn afterward.
